Chapter 12 · A Physicist's History of Bad Philosophy

Bad philosophyforbids asking

Bad philosophy doesn't just get things wrong. It stops people looking for better explanations. In quantum physics, a rule against asking what is really happening held back the explanation for decades — while the predictions kept working, so few noticed what was lost.

The example: the math of quantum theory, found in the 1920s, worked perfectly. Asking what it described was ruled out. When Everett answered in 1957, the answer was shelved — not refuted by any experiment.

Real life

Many worlds, shelved. Everett's 1957 thesis took quantum theory at its word. Physicists largely ignored it for years.

Don't ask. The Copenhagen view of Bohr told physicists to stop at the measurements. Asking what is really happening was treated as out of bounds.
